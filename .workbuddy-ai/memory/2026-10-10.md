## Cropping forensics — he was right, the images WERE cut (2026-10-10, late)

He insisted the crops must be checked properly ("اكو نقوصات مثلا انو الخط او الكتابة تتقطع") and told me to keep doubting everything until it is 100%. He was right on both counts.

**What was wrong, and how each was found:**
1. **The OCR text layer of this scan is offset ~4 pt from the printed ink** (measured +3.5/+4.2/+4.5 on three sample words). So *any* crop boundary derived from text coordinates cuts glyphs. **Fix:** stop trusting text coordinates for cutting — render a generous band and snap the edge to the **nearest blank row**, measured over the **text column only** (a full-bleed photo otherwise touches every row and hides every gap).
2. **Nearest** blank row, not "walk in one direction": walking up from a bound that sits inside the previous line's ink drags that whole line into the crop. That single change fixed the neighbouring-section bleed.
3. **Two exercises were genuinely truncated** because my hand-picked last anchor was not the real last line: `e01` stopped after Unit-Opener sub-question 1 (missing 2 and 3) and `e14` stopped after MAIN IDEAS item 1 (missing items 2-4). Both found by listing the text inside each crop box and reading it.
4. **`e04` cut the last shopper line** ("buying them.") because `BOTTOM_LIMIT = 680` was above the real last line at ~705. Raised to 712, then tightened to 706 so the page footer stays out.
5. **`e23` (brainstorm) was missing steps 1-3**, which live on the next page.
6. A column-profile bug: I sliced the column-sum array with **row** indices (`colink[t:b+1]`), which produced absurd 28-pt-wide crops. Fixed by summing ink over the row band instead.

**Method that actually works (reusable):** list the text inside each crop box (`page.get_text("blocks")` filtered by the box) and read it — that catches a *missing* exercise instantly. Then assert the **boundary rows are blank**, which is what proves no glyph is sliced. Ink-on-edge counts alone cannot tell a bleed photo from cut text.
