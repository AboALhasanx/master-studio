## ASE — he rejected "scan + OCR layer" and proposed a real digitisation (chapter by chapter → Word)

His idea: don't ship a scan with a text layer — **rebuild the book as text**, chapter by chapter, and export to Word.

**Verdict: the idea is right, and I proved the pipeline works.**

Built `02_Raw_Materials/Aggarwal_SE_3rdEd_COMPLETE/_digitizer/digitize_chapter.py` (pilot v3). Pipeline per page range of the merged PDF:
- `get_text('dict')` blocks → paragraph units
- strip running heads (`Software Engineering`, chapter title) and bare folios
- de-hyphenate across line breaks, normalise bullets (`e ` → `• `) and quotes (`·x·` → `“x”`)
- **figures:** a scanned page is ONE full-page image, so image blocks are useless for finding figures. Anchor on the **caption line** (`Fig. 5.1: …`) — crop everything above it as the figure, then keep only text below the caption. If no caption and the page is mostly noise, crop the whole content area.
- drop short high-junk blocks (diagram label OCR)

**Sample result (Ch5, PDF pp.223–227):** prose ~95 % usable ("Design is the highly significant phase in the software development where the designer plans “how”…"), and Fig. 5.1 cropped perfectly as a 200 dpi PNG with its caption. Saved as `_digitizer/sample_Ch05_223-227.md` + `figs/`.
