## STUDY — "annotated mirror translation" format (student's new idea; DISCUSSION ONLY, nothing built)
- **Student's model:** take a doctor's booklet/chapter and produce a document that is, per section/sub-paragraph, THREE layers together: ① the source text reproduced **verbatim with its original formatting** (like a screenshot / faithful facsimile, not a re-summary), ② a **literal Arabic translation**, ③ a **comprehension explanation** (شرح فهمي) — bilingual, even if the page count ≈ doubles.
- **His bachelor reference doc he showed me:** `C:\Users\gokoq\Downloads\Telegram Desktop\قواعد بيانات موزعة-جابتر1 مترجم.pdf` (Distributed Databases, Dr. Usama Almusawi, Wasit). 49 pages. Confirmed structure by rendering pages: **page-for-page mirror** — page N = English original with its exact header ("Distributed Databases by Dr. Usama Almusawi"), figure, and page number; page N+1 = Arabic translation with the *same* header, *same* section numbers, *same* figure images duplicated verbatim. **He made it by hand in Word — tiring but very useful.** Its one weakness (his own complaint): the Arabic is **pure literal translation, zero comprehension layer** — that's the exact gap his new idea closes.
- **Feasibility verdict I gave:** YES, achievable, and it is the *right* direction — but split the deliverable: **(A) verbatim source facsimile** (must be a faithful copy — do NOT let an LLM "reflow" text; copy the page/image or the exact extracted text + original styling) + **(B) translation + comprehension** (LLM-authored). The precision the student wants in layer ① is precisely what an LLM is *worst* at and a copy/paste is *perfect* at. Vault already has: `pdf_exporter.py` (Playwright/Chromium, bidi, `--lang bilingual`, `tag_bilingual_blocks`, 4 presets) → the rendering engine exists; the missing piece is the *facing-page* two-layer layout + an image-duplication step.
- **Research evidence gathered (for the "is it actually better?" question):**
  - **"Thick translation"** (Appiah 1993, translation studies): translation + annotations/glosses/commentary — the established scholarly term for exactly layer ③. Cheung (2010) supplied the *methods* Appiah left unspecified.
  - **L1 use in L2 reading:** ERIC EJ1358393 (2023) + L1/L2 reading-comprehension integrative work (2019) — translation into the native language *supports* reading comprehension; it is not a crutch to avoid.
  - **Elaborative interrogation / self-explanation** (generative learning literature): prompting a *why/how* explanation alongside the text is one of the highest-utility, most underused techniques → this is the scientific justification for layer ③ (شرح فهمي).
  - **Layout-preserving side-by-side bilingual readers exist** (zotero-bilingual / PDF2zh / BabelDOC, Immersive Translate) — but they do **translation only**, sentence-aligned, no comprehension layer → confirms the three-layer idea is a genuine gap, not a re-invention.
- **"System 1 fast decision-making" concern (his own framing):** the risk is the comprehension layer collapsing into a generic LLM re-summary (filler) instead of a real per-paragraph explanation; and over-triggering (every tiny sentence getting 3 layers = bloat). Verdict: keep it but budget it — the comprehension layer should be *earned* (added where the paragraph carries a concept/equation/list, thin where it's already trivial).
