## 4. القاعدة — Articles

> **في سطر:** a/an لأول ذكر غير محدد · the للمعروف/الوحيد · Ø للجمع العام وغير المعدود العام.

| الحالة | الأداة | مثال |
|:--|:--|:--|
| اسم معدود مفرد، أول ذكر | a/an | I bought **a** table. |
| اسم معدود مفرد، معروف | the | **The** table is in the kitchen. |
| اسم وحيد | the | **the** Internet · **the** weather |
| جمع عام | Ø | **Ø** Buyers want good prices. |
| غير معدود عام | Ø | **Ø** Information is important. |

| ❌ خطأ | ✅ صحيح | السبب |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **a** information | **Ø** information | غير معدود |
| **an** university | **a** university | صوت /j/ |
| **the** life is difficult | **Ø** Life is difficult | عام |
| I go to **the** school (للدراسة) | I go to **Ø** school | تعبير ثابت |

## 5. الكتابة — Descriptive Essay

**الهيكل:**

1. **Introduction** — جذب الانتباه + thesis statement.
2. **Body 1** — الميزة الأولى + تفاصيل حسية.
3. **Body 2** — الميزة الثانية + تفاصيل حسية.
4. **Conclusion** — الرأي النهائي.

**الحواس:** sight · sound · smell · taste · touch.

**مثال thesis:**
> The small bakery on our street is the best place for breakfast because of its freshness, its prices, and its atmosphere.

# القواعد — Articles: a / an / the / Ø

> **القاعدة في سطر:** a/an لأول ذكر غير محدد · the للمعروف أو الوحيد · Ø للجمع العام وغير المعدود العام.

---

## 1. الخريطة

| النوع | مفرد معدود | جمع معدود | غير معدود |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **غير محدد** | a / an | Ø | Ø |
| **محدد** | the | the | the |

**أمثلة:**

- I bought **a** table. *(أول ذكر)*
- **The** table is in the kitchen. *(معروف)*
- **Ø** Tables are expensive. *(جمع عام)*
- **Ø** Information is important. *(غير معدود عام)*

---

## 2. a / an — حسب الصوت، مو الحرف

| الصوت | الأداة | أمثلة |
|:--|:--|:--|
| صوت ساكن | **a** | a book · a university (/juː/) · a European · a one-way street (/w/) |
| صوت علة | **an** | an apple · an hour (h صامتة) · an MBA (/em/) · an honest man |

| ❌ خطأ | ✅ صحيح | السبب |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **an** university | **a** university | صوت /j/ |
| **a** hour | **an** hour | h صامتة |
| **an** European | **a** European | صوت /j/ |

---

## 3. the — متى؟

1. **ذُكر سابقاً:** I bought a table. **The** table is nice.
2. **معروف من السياق:** **The** kitchen is small. *(مطبخ البيت)*
3. **وحيد:** **the** Internet · **the** weather · **the** government · **the** sun.
4. **صفة عليا:** **the** best · **the** most important.
5. **أسماء جغرافية:** **the** Tigris · **the** Gulf · **the** United States. *(لكن: Iraq, Baghdad, Mount Everest بلا the)*
6. **جملة وصل تحدد الاسم:** **the** job **that I told you about**.

---

## 4. Ø — متى؟

1. **جمع عام:** **Ø** Buyers want good prices.
2. **غير معدود عام:** **Ø** Information is useful. · **Ø** Life is difficult.
3. **أسماء العلم:** **Ø** Iraq · **Ø** Baghdad · **Ø** English.
4. **الوجبات/الرياضة/المواد:** **Ø** breakfast · **Ø** football · **Ø** mathematics.
5. **تعبيرات ثابتة:** go to **Ø** school (للدراسة) · go to **Ø** bed · at **Ø** home · at **Ø** work · by **Ø** car.

> **ملاحظة:** go to **the** school = تروح للمبنى، مو للدراسة.

## Reading 1 — So Much Dead Space

### 1. قبل القراءة

**المؤلف:** Paco Underhill — باحث في سلوك التسوّق.
**السؤال:** ليش واجهات المحلات صارت "مساحة ميتة"؟

### 2. ملخص عربي

الكاتب باحث أمريكي يدرس سلوك التسوّق من 25 سنة. يقول إن واجهات المحلات صارت **"مساحة ميتة" (dead space)** لأن المشاة اليوم يمشون بسرعة وينظرون إلى الأمام، فلا يتوقفون أمام الواجهة. قبل 100 سنة كان الناس يمشون ببطء ويتوقفون لرؤية أحدث الأزياء.

الواجهة اليوم لازم تكون **"quick read"**: بسيطة بما يكفي لتمييز المنتجات، ومبدعة بما يكفي لجذب عين الماشي. الواجهة المزدحمة (مثل واجهة الصيدلية) تجعل من المستحيل التركيز على منتج واحد.

الكاتب يذكر **نمط الحشود والفراغات**: الناس يتجمعون عند إشارات المرور، ثم تتشكل فجوة، ثم حشد جديد. هذا النمط يؤثر على من يرى الواجهة.

**أمثلة ناجحة:** واجهة Kiehl's تستخدم قضايا اجتماعية تناسب زبائنها؛ ومحل في ستراسبورغ يروي نكاتاً وقصصاً تاريخية، ويميّز نفسه عن الآخرين.

## 2. القصة

Every afternoon, Noor walked home from school along Al-Nahr Street. The street was **urban** and crowded, and **pedestrians** moved quickly past the shops. But Noor always stopped in front of one window.

It was **a** small shop that sold dresses. **The** window was simple: one dress, **a** pair of shoes, and **a** mirror. **The** dress was deep blue, and **the** light made it look soft and expensive. Noor fell in love with it.

She saved money for three months. She did not buy coffee, and she walked to school instead of taking the bus. Her friends thought she was crazy. “It’s just **a** dress,” they said. But Noor had **a** different **concept**: the dress was not just cloth. It was **a** version of herself that she wanted to become.

Finally, she had enough money. She went to the shop and bought **the** dress. The owner smiled and said, “You have good taste.” Noor took **the** dress home and put it on. But when she looked in the mirror, she felt disappointed. **The** color was not as deep as it looked in the window. **The** fabric was thin, and **the** shape was not special.

She went back to the shop the next day. The owner was not surprised. “**The** window has special lights,” he said. “And **the** mirror makes **the** dress look bigger. Every shop does this.”

Noor felt angry at first. Then she became curious. She started to **focus** on **the** way shops **promoted** their products. She read about **the** psychology of shopping. She learned that **a** **sophisticated** window display can make **an** **individual** feel that the product will change her life. She learned that shops **distinguish** themselves by creating **a** mood, not just by selling things.

Over time, Noor’s **priorities** **evolved**. She stopped buying things because of **a** window. She started asking, “Do I need this, or does **the** shop want me to need it?” She realized that understanding advertising can **liberate** you. It does not mean you never buy anything. It means you choose with open eyes.

Today, Noor still walks on Al-Nahr Street. She still looks at **the** window with **the** blue dress. But now she smiles. She knows the difference between **a** beautiful display and **a** good purchase.

# قطعة خارجية — Why We Buy Things We Don't Need

> **قطعة أصلية** بأسئلة بصيغة الامتحان. الوقت المقترح: 15 دقيقة.

---

## 1. اقرأ القطعة

Have you ever walked into a store for one thing and walked out with three? Most people have. Scientists who study consumer behavior say that our decisions are not as rational as we think.

One reason is **scarcity**. When a product is limited, we feel that we must buy it quickly. A sign that says “Only 3 left!” creates **a** feeling of urgency. **The** product may be ordinary, but **the** message makes it feel special. **The** same thing happens with time-limited sales: “Today only!” pushes people to decide before they are ready.

Another reason is **social proof**. We look at what other people are doing, especially when we are not sure. If **a** restaurant is full, we assume **the** food is good. If **a** product has many positive reviews, we trust it more. **The** power of social proof is strong, even when the reviews are not real.

**A** third reason is **the** design of the store. **The** lights, **the** music, and **the** smell are not accidents. They are chosen to make us stay longer and feel comfortable. **A** pleasant environment can make **an** ordinary product seem more valuable. This is why **the** best stores **create a mood** before they sell **a** product.

Understanding these techniques does not mean we should never buy anything. It means we should **focus** on our **priorities**. Before you buy, ask yourself three questions: Do I need this? Why do I want it now? Would I still want it tomorrow? These questions can **liberate** you from **the** pressure of advertising.

## 2. موديل — مقال وصفي

> **The Small Bakery on Our Street**
>
> The small bakery on the corner of our street is my favourite place to buy breakfast because of its freshness, its prices, and its atmosphere. It is not a famous shop, and it does not have a big sign, but everyone in the neighbourhood knows it.
>
> As soon as you open the door, **the** rich smell of bread fills the air. **The** shop is small and warm, with **a** wooden counter and **a** few old shelves. **The** baker works behind **the** counter, and you can hear **the** soft sound of **the** oven and **the** quiet hum of **an** old radio. **The** bread is baked three times a day, so **the** loaves are always warm. When you touch them, **the** crust is crisp, and **the** inside is soft and light.
>
> **The** prices are another reason I keep coming back. **A** full breakfast — bread, cheese, and tea — costs less than **a** cup of coffee in **the** city centre. **The** owner knows his customers by name, and he always asks about **the** family. This creates **a** friendly mood that you cannot find in **a** large supermarket. **The** only negative is that **the** shop closes at two o'clock, so you cannot buy bread in the evening.
>
> In short, **the** small bakery is not just **a** place to buy food. It is **a** place where **the** community meets. Anyone who wants fresh bread and **a** warm welcome should stop, take **a** look, and go in. **The** smell alone is worth **the** trip.
