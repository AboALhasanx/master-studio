# قصة قصيرة — The First Spelling Bee

> **قصة أصلية** للتدريب. تستخدم مفردات الوحدة وpast perfect. ليست من الكتاب.

## 2. القصة

When I was nine, my teacher **selected** me for the school spelling bee. I was surprised. I had never won anything before, and I **assumed** that I would lose in the first round. My **assumption** was based on fear, not on facts.

My mother did not laugh at me. Instead, she decided to **nurture** my confidence. Every evening after dinner, we sat at the kitchen table with a list of words. She read the word, I spelled it, and she wrote down my mistakes. We did this for six weeks. By the time the competition began, I **had studied** more than 200 words.

My mother believed that **competence** is not **innate**. “Nobody is born a good speller,” she said. “You become one through practice.” She was **consistent with** her words: she never missed a single evening, even when she was tired.

The day of the competition arrived. The hall was full of students, parents, and teachers. I wore my best shirt and carried a small notebook **equipped with** all my words. I was nervous, but I felt ready.

The first rounds were easy. I spelled “**adolescence**,” “**significance**,” and “**extracurricular**” **accurately**. Then the judge asked me to spell “**rhythm**.” I froze. I had seen the word, but I had never learned its strange spelling. I took a breath and said, “R-H-Y-T-M.” I was wrong. I had forgotten the second “h.”

I left the stage feeling **resentful**. I had worked so hard, and I had lost. My mother hugged me and said, “This **period** of your life is not about winning. It is about learning how to work.” I did not understand her then. I understand her now.

That experience taught me a lesson that I have never forgotten: **talent** may be innate, but **competence** is built. The competition was not the end of my learning; it was the beginning. Today, when I teach, I tell my students the same thing: “You are not born ready. You become ready.”

## 3. أسئلة الفهم

### A) Main Idea

1. What is the main idea of the story?
   a) Spelling bees are unfair.
   b) A mother helps her child learn that competence comes from practice.
   c) The narrator is the best speller in school.
   d) Teachers are too strict.

### B) Details

2. Why was the narrator surprised when he was selected?
3. What did his mother do every evening?
4. How many words had he studied before the competition?
5. Which word did he spell wrong?
6. What did his mother say after he lost?

### C) Inference

7. Why did the narrator freeze on the word “rhythm”?
8. Why did he feel resentful?
9. What does “You are not born ready. You become ready” mean?

### D) Vocabulary in Context

10. Find a word that means “a belief without proof”.
11. Find a word that means “present from birth”.
12. Find a word that means “the ability to do something well”.

### E) Grammar — Past Perfect

13. Underline the past perfect: “I had studied more than 200 words.”
14. Why is past perfect used here?
15. Combine: “I studied 200 words. Then the competition began.” (by the time)

### F) Writing

16. Write a paragraph (80–100 words) about a time you failed at something and what you learned.

## 4. الإجابات

**A)** 1-b

**B)**

2. Because he had never won anything and assumed he would lose.
3. She practiced spelling with him at the kitchen table.
4. More than 200 words.
5. “Rhythm.”
6. She said the period was not about winning; it was about learning how to work.

**C)**

7. Because he had seen the word but had never learned its unusual spelling.
8. Because he had worked hard and still lost.
9. Competence is not innate; it is built through practice and experience.

**D)** 10. assumption · 11. innate · 12. competence

**E)**

13. **had studied**
14. لأن الدراسة صارت قبل بداية المسابقة (earlier event).
15. **By the time the competition began, I had studied 200 words.**

**F) نموذج:**

> When I was in middle school, I failed my first English exam. I had studied for two days, but I had not understood the grammar. I felt **resentful** because I had worked hard. My teacher told me that failure is a **period** of learning, not the end. I changed my method: I practiced every day and asked questions. The next exam was much better. I learned that **competence** is built, not **innate**.
