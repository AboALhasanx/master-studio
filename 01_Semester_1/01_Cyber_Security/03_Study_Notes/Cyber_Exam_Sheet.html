Week 01 — the six equations

#EquationMeaning and symbols
1min R = ΣPiIi − ΣCj Cybersecurity as an optimisation problem. Minimise residual risk = expected loss (Σ probability × impact) minus the cost of controls. Spend on a control only while it removes more loss than it costs.
2R(t) = ΣPi(t) Ii(t) Expected risk at time t, summed over all threats. Awareness only — the doctor said you just need to know the law exists.
3U(C,I,A) = αC + βI + γA Security utility function. C, I, A normalised 0–1; α, β, γ are the importance weights for that system. Weights are not fixed — see the domain table below.
4AS = Σ(Ej · Vj · Aj) Attack surface. Ej exposed entry points · Vj vulnerability severity (CVSS score) · Aj asset value. A product, not a sum — all three must matter.
5P(R > r) = 1 − F(r) Probability that risk exceeds a threshold r. F is a CDF, so its range is always [0, 1]; 1 − F(r) gives the tail probability.
6PCI = Σwkck / Σwk Policy compliance index — a weighted average. ck compliance level for requirement k (0–1); wk its importance weight. Result 0–1 (×100 for a percentage).

Week 02 — Risk · Exam Sheet

Booklet 2 · from Sharp, Introduction to Cybersecurity, Ch. 3, pp. 37–56

The two equations

EquationSymbols
S = F × K S basic risk · F frequency of attempts to exploit the vulnerability · K consequences of a successful attempt
R = S / M R residual risk · S risk of the threat · M level of countermeasures — number AND effectiveness
How to use them The scale is ordinal (low / medium / high) and the book's own examples print “medium × high = medium”. Read the colour off the matrix — do not multiply the words as numbers.
MatrixAxesRed corner
Risk matrixfrequency × consequenceshigh and high
Residual risk matrixrisk ÷ countermeasureshigh risk, low countermeasures
Why multiply, not add Multiplication collapses when either factor is near zero — a constant nuisance that harms nothing is not a catastrophe. Addition would let a huge frequency compensate for zero consequence, which is nonsense. Both factors must matter.

Traps, in order

  1. A threat is not a vulnerability. Weakness alone = vulnerability; actor alone = threat.
  2. Multiplication, not addition (AS and S = F × K).
  3. Red has exactly one cell in each matrix.
  4. Do not mix the matrices.
  5. M is number AND effectiveness — not just a count.
  6. The two five-item lists differ — dealing with damage vs mitigation strategies.
  7. Proactive 1–2–3, reactive 4–5.
  8. Act restarts PDCA — a cycle, not a line.
  9. OCTAVE phases 3, variants 4.
  10. Authentication factors = 4 — most people forget Location.
  11. Authorisation is based on authentication.
  12. Insider threats → administrative controls (vetting, training), not technology.
  13. Three remote terms differ by word order — monitoring · remote-access control · remote-control access.
