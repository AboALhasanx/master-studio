## Retrieval set — T5 Evaluate

**E.1 · Is software engineering an art, a craft, or a science? Judge and justify your position.**
{: .keep-next }

> [!NOTE] Answer
> Software engineering is best described as an **engineering discipline**: systematic in its principles and still dependent on design judgement. The **art of programming alone is no longer enough to build large programs**, and software engineering is an important discipline of study, practice and research.
>
> **Aggarwal, §1.2.1, p.4; §1.6.4, p.16.**

---

**E.2 · Which type of software product accounts for more development projects — generic or customised? Evaluate and justify.**
{: .keep-next }

> [!NOTE] Answer
> **Generic products** are **"developed for anonymous customers"** — the target is **"the entire world"** and many copies are sold (operating systems, compilers, word processors, CASE tools). **Customised products** are **"developed for particular customers"** to their specific requirements. Judging by development volume, customised is larger: **"most of the development projects (say about 80%) come under this category."**
>
> **Aggarwal, §1.5.7, p.14.**
> [!WARN] دقّق بالوحدة قبل ما تحكم
> الـ80% نسبة **من المشاريع المطوَّرة**، مو من المستخدمين ولا من النسخ. المنتج generic يُباع «للعالم كله» بنسخ كثيرة فيوصل مستخدمين أكثر.

---

**E.3 · What is more important: product or process? Justify your judgement.**
{: .keep-next }

> [!NOTE] Answer
> **"An efficient process is required to produce good quality products. If the process is weak, the end product will undoubtedly suffer, but an obsessive over-reliance on process is also dangerous."** The aim is to focus **"on both the quality of the product and on the process used to develop the product"**, and the dependency order puts process **"after people and product."**
>
> Neither is universally more important: an effective process supports product quality, while excessive reliance on process can be harmful. The right balance depends on the product and its requirements.
>
> **Aggarwal, §1.5.2, p.13; §1.6.3, p.16; Fig. 1.8, p.15.**
