### Question 2.2
**Which model is most popular for student’s small projects?**
- (a) Waterfall model
- (b) Spiral model
- (c) Quick and fix model
- (d) Prototyping model

<!-- Print-hidden label: Correct Answer --> **(c) Quick and fix model**

**Technical Rationale:** **The quick and fix model (also referred to as Build & Fix) is the most popular approach for small student programming exercises of 100 to 200 lines because code is written immediately without requiring formal specifications or architectural design.**

> **ملاحظة الطالب والفخ الامتحاني:**
> - يُطلق عليه في مراجع أخرى Build and Fix؛ هو أسلوب عفوي (Ad-hoc) يناسب التمارين الفردية الصغيرة ولا يصلح للمشاريع الحقيقية.

### Question 2.3
**Which is not a software life cycle model?**
- (a) Waterfall model
- (b) Spiral model
- (c) Prototyping model
- (d) Capability maturity model

<!-- Print-hidden label: Correct Answer --> **(d) Capability maturity model**

**Technical Rationale:** **Waterfall, Spiral, and Prototyping are software development process/life-cycle models defining phases of development.** **The Capability Maturity Model (CMM) is an organizational process improvement and maturity appraisal framework developed by the SEI, not an SDLC process model.**

> **ملاحظة الطالب والفخ الامتحاني:**
> - نموذج CMM يقيّم نضج المنظمة البرمجية ككل (Maturity Framework)، ولا يحدد مراحل بناء البرمجيات كدورة حياة.

*Source: K.K. Aggarwal & Singh (2007) Slide 41 (Q2.3).*

## Retrieval set — Focused Active Recall Drill

**1. What fundamental distinction separates an SDLC process model from an organizational framework like CMM?**
> **An SDLC process model (such as Waterfall, Spiral, or RAD) prescribes the chronological phases, technical activities, and deliverables for engineering software.** In contrast, the Capability Maturity Model (CMM) is an organizational appraisal framework that assesses the maturity and process management capability of the organization as a whole, rather than governing development phases.
>
> *Source: K.K. Aggarwal & Singh (2007) Slide 41 (Q2.3).*

**2. Why does the Waterfall model require requirements to be completely defined and frozen before design and coding commence?**
> **Because development is structured to proceed strictly linearly without backward iteration.** Once a phase is completed, subsequent stages depend entirely on its baseline deliverables, making late requirement changes disruptive and difficult to accommodate within a linear cascade.
>
> *Source: K.K. Aggarwal & Singh (2007) Slides 6, 8, 9; Slide 42 (Q2.8); Slide 44 (Q2.20).*
