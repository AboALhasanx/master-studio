## Retrieval set

> **Cover the answer, produce your own, then compare.** Each item points at a question in Aggarwal & Singh, *Software Engineering* (3rd ed., New Age International, 2007); the page number is that of the exercise itself. The answers below are concise concept statements written from standard SE principles — not verbatim textbook quotations. [Foundational Knowledge / Standard Concept]

**1. The primary goal of development is shifting from "good quality software" to "good quality maintainable software". Why? (Ch.1, p.61, Q1.1)**
> Because a software's value now outlives its first release. Hardware is cheap and replaceable, but the system keeps being modified for years, so the cost that dominates is **evolution, not construction**. A program that is excellent but undebuggable becomes a liability the moment the requirements change. *التعليل: كل تغيير مستقبلي يدفع ثمن صعوبة التعديل، والجودة بدون قابلية للصيانة تنطفئ مع أول طلب تعديل.*

**2. Why are CASE tools normally unable to control the software crisis? (Ch.1, p.61, Q1.2)**
> CASE automates the **mechanics** of building (diagramming, code generation, version control) but cannot repair a broken **process or requirements**. The crisis originates in estimation, communication and scope — decisions the tool does not own. *التعليل: الأداة تنظّم العمل، ما تصلح فشل القرار.*

**3. Was Y2K a software crisis? Justify. (Ch.1, p.61, Q1.4)**
> Y2K was the **symptom that exposed** the crisis rather than the crisis itself: a defect known for decades, left unfixed because maintenance was perpetually under-prioritised and rewrites were deferred as "not urgent". It is the maintainability failure of Q1.1 arriving at national scale — huge remediation cost for code that still had to be discarded or rewritten. *التعليل: الأزمة ليست الخطأ، بل استحالة معالجته بأثر رخيص.*

**4. Illustrate that software does not wear out, and state the consequence. (Ch.1, p.62, Q1.12–1.13)**
> Software has no physical wear mechanism, so its failure curve does not rise from ageing — it fails only because of **latent defects surfacing** or because the **environment changed** around it (hardware, OS, inputs, regulation). The consequence is inverted from hardware: degradation must be fought with **preventive maintenance**, and "old" software is not automatically untrustworthy. *التعليل: العتاد يهرم، والبرمجيات تتقادم متطلباتها.*

**5. Is software a product, a process, or both? Which matters more? (Ch.1, p.62–64, Q1.16 / Q1.23 / Q1.25)**
> It is **both**: the *product* is the deliverable (program + data + documentation), the *process* is the ordered set of activities that produced it. **Process matters more**, because a sound process tolerates an imperfect team while an unsound process fails even with a strong one — the product is an output of the process, not an independent achievement. *التعليل: النتيجة قابلة للتحسين فقط عبر مصدرها، والبرنامج بلا المستندات نصف منتج.*

**6. Why must a team developing a large product adhere to a life cycle model? (Ch.2, p.46, Q2.1)**
> Because scale destroys ad-hoc coordination. A life cycle model fixes **the order of activities, the entry/exit criteria of each phase and the artefacts exchanged**, so progress is measurable and defects are found near their origin rather than at delivery. For small work build-and-fix can hide the disorder; at large scale the disorder shows up as rework. *التعليل: النموذج هو عقد التنسيق بين الفريق والمراجع.*

**7. List the advantages of waterfall over the ad-hoc build-and-fix model. (Ch.2, p.46, Q2.3)**
> Waterfall gives **explicit milestones and deliverables**, forces requirements to be settled before coding, makes review and estimation possible, and produces the documentation later phases depend on. Build-and-fix offers no checkpoint at which anyone can say what "done" means — which is precisely why it collapses on non-trivial systems. *التعليل: بدون مراحل محدّدة لا يمكن قياس التقدم ولا اكتشاف الخطأ مبكراً.*

**8. What is the effect of designing a prototype on the overall project cost? (Ch.2, p.46, Q2.4)**
> It **raises cost in the short term and reduces it overall**: the throw-away prototype is extra effort, but it converts an unknown (misunderstood requirements, unproven feasibility) into a known one **before** full-scale construction, when changes are still cheap. The cost of discovering a requirements error in construction or operation is an order of magnitude higher. *التعليل: اكتشف الخطأ في الرسم قبل ما تكتشفه بالبناء.*

**9. Compare the waterfall and spiral models, and explain what the outward spiral path implies about project risk. (Ch.2, p.47, Q2.9 / Q2.10 / Q2.11)**
> Waterfall is **linear and single-pass**: plan → specify → build → test, with each phase feeding the next. Spiral is **risk-driven and cyclic**: every loop adds an increment and explicitly evaluates **project risk** before committing to more work. Moving outward means the product is being extended while the *residual* risk falls — each cycle retires the riskiest assumption first, so the software matures from "feasible" to "complete" instead of running once toward an uncertain end. *التعليل: الشلال يفترض أن المتطلبات صحيحة، واللولب يفترض العكس ويختبره.*

**10. Requirements analysis is the most communication-intensive step in software engineering — why does the communication path frequently break down? (Ch.3, p.134, Q3.2)**
> Because the two ends speak different languages and hold different mental models: users describe **problems in domain terms** (vague, exception-laden, context assumed), analysts must convert them into **unambiguous, testable specifications**. The path breaks at the unstated assumptions — nobody volunteers what they consider obvious, so gaps and contradictions survive into analysis undetected. *التعليل: الانقطاع يصير بالفترات التي يفترض فيها كل طرف أن الطرف الآخر فاهم.*

**11. Which of these statements are ambiguous, and why? (Ch.3, p.138, Q3.29)**
> **(a) "good response time"** — ambiguous: no measurable bound, and "good" differs by context. **(b) "menu driven"** — ambiguous: no menu structure, depth or navigation specified. **(c) "twenty-five buttons PF1–PF25"** and **(d) "shall not exceed 128K of RAM"** — both **verifiable**: a reviewer can count the buttons and measure the footprint. The test is *could two independent testers disagree on pass/fail?* If yes, the requirement is not yet specified. *التعليل: أي جملة تقبل قراءتين تُسقط قابلية التحقق، والتحقق شرط القبول.*

**12. Why is the SRS known as the black box specification of a system? (Ch.3, p.139, Q3.31)**
> Because it specifies **externally observable behaviour** — inputs, outputs, functions, performance and constraints — while saying nothing about **how** the system implements them internally. That abstraction is deliberate: it lets the customer validate the contract without being locked to an implementation, and lets design freedom remain with the developers. *التعليل: يعرّف "ماذا" ويترك "كيف" حراً — عقد قابل للفحص دون تقييد التصميم.*
