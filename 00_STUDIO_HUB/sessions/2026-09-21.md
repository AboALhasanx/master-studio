- Student correctly understood that a crisp binary threshold can be brittle: a small change around the boundary can flip the decision completely.
- Distinguished `Uncertainty` from `Imprecision / Vagueness`:
  - Uncertainty: the value itself is not known precisely, such as `38°C ± 1°C`.
  - Imprecision/Vagueness: the value is known, but the linguistic boundary is not sharp, such as whether `38°C` is `Warm` or `Hot`.
- Student passed the conceptual check and is ready to continue with the four lecture properties: Tolerance of Imprecision, Uncertainty, Robustness, and Low Solution Cost.
- For Tolerance of Imprecision, the student correctly identified that an approximate route can be successful when it reaches the goal, even if a faster or better route may exist. The acceptable decision depends on constraints such as error bounds, time, cost, and risk; approximate does not automatically mean optimal.
