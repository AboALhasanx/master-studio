## Source identified + fact-checked against public sources (2026-09-23, later)

The student asked for internet verification of the Risk material rather than continued reliance on the lecture and the booklet. Done.

- **SOURCE IDENTIFIED:** the Cyber Week 02 Risk booklet is a **direct extraction of Chapter 3 of a published Springer textbook** — **Sharp, R., "Risk", in *Introduction to Cybersecurity: A Multidisciplinary Challenge*, Undergraduate Topics in Computer Science, Springer, Cham, 2024, pp. 37–56**, DOI [10.1007/978-3-031-41463-3_3](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-41463-3_3). Author: **Robin Sharp, emeritus professor, Cybersecurity Section, DTU Compute (Denmark)** — which explains the booklet's "liveware" term and its `K`/`M` letters. Identification evidence: a verbatim match on the booklet's opening sentence, the stray `3` being the chapter number, the figure numbering (3.1–3.3), and the chapter abstract matching the booklet's three opening movements.
- **Deliverable:** `03_Study_Notes/Week_02_Risk_Source_Verification.md` — every claim traced to a named source with a link.
- **TWO VERIFIED FACTUAL ERRORS in the booklet:**
  1. **"ISO/IEC 27002 from 2022 … 14 categories" is wrong.** The 14 categories are the **2013** structure (114 controls in 14 clauses); the **2022** version is **4 themes / 93 controls**. The booklet's 14-item list matches the 2013 clause set **verbatim and in order**. **This is the line Dr. Huda highlighted yellow** — so it is exam material.
  2. **"OCTAVE FORTE" does not exist.** Only **three** public OCTAVE methodologies: OCTAVE, OCTAVE-S, OCTAVE Allegro. (Also: OCTAVE Allegro runs 8 steps in 4 phases, not 3.)
- **Terminology issue:** the authentication factor printed as **"Inheritance"** should be **"Inherence"**; the core standard set is knowledge / possession / inherence, with **location** as an extension rather than part of the classic triad.
- **Imprecision:** OCTAVE was developed at CMU in 2001 **for the US Department of Defense**; CERT/SEI is the CMU body. The booklet's "developed for the international organization CERT" is close but wrong on the sponsor and on CERT's status.
- **Confirmed correct:** OCTAVE's three phases (verbatim), PDCA, the five mitigation strategies, the four threat groups, the risk definition and the objective/subjective distinction.
- **Could not be checked:** Sharp's exact equation letters — the chapter body is **paywalled**. The booklet is proven to be a direct extraction, so `S = F × K` and `R = S / M` are almost certainly Sharp's. Also recorded that the mainstream industry formula for residual risk is **subtraction** (Residual Risk = Inherent Risk − Control Effectiveness), so the division form is Sharp's own modelling choice, not a universal convention.
- **Exam strategy recorded:** answer in the doctor's vocabulary (14 categories, four OCTAVE variants) because she marks it, but know the correct facts and state them parenthetically where there is room. That converts a fact-check into extra marks rather than a risk of contradicting the professor.
