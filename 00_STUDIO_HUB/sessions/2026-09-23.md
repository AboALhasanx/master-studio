## Mathematical verification of the two equations (2026-09-23, later)

The student asked for the equations to be verified "mathematically and logically" and suggested looking for published page images. Done — and the maths settled it without the paywalled text.

- **Image search: failed, and reported as failed.** Springer's chapter and front-matter PDFs are paywalled and behind a bot challenge; exact-phrase search on the chapter's own sentences returns only the landing page; the chapter body is not publicly indexed; no Google Books preview surfaced. The only usable fragment was a figure caption confirming a **3-point low/medium/high scale** and **two matrices**. Recorded honestly rather than papered over.
- **New deliverable:** `03_Study_Notes/Week_02_Risk_Formula_Mathematical_Verification.md`.
- **`S = F × K` — proven sound.** Orthodox likelihood × consequence form. **Multiplication is required, not addition**, because addition would let a huge frequency compensate for zero consequence. On the book's 3-point scale the product takes only six distinct values (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9), so the matrix is **symmetric and non-uniformly spaced** — which is exactly why colour banding is necessary rather than a raw number.
- **`R = S / M` — proven sound only on `M ≥ 1`.** Boundary analysis: `M → ∞ ⇒ R → 0` (correct); `M = 1 ⇒ R = S` (correct, requires normalisation); **`M = 0` divides by zero** (wrong — no defence must give `R = S`); **`0 < M < 1` gives `R > S`** (impossible). Therefore **`M` cannot be a raw count** — which is precisely why the booklet's "number *and* effectiveness" wording is necessary, not padding. The maths and the book's sentence agree.
- **Equivalence proof:** substituting `M = 1/(1 − CE)` into `R = S/M` yields `S(1 − CE)` — the industry-standard residual-risk formula exactly. **The division form is the same model as the mainstream one, re-parameterised.** Both boundaries match (`CE = 0 ⇒ M = 1 ⇒ R = S`; `CE → 1 ⇒ M → ∞ ⇒ R → 0`).
- **The student's reported version fails a logical test.** With `f` as the subject of both equations, `s` would have to mean "frequency" in one and "risk" in the other — a contradiction. And a denominator of *number of threats* fails twice: more threats would lower risk, and zero threats would divide by zero. **A defensive quantity belongs in the denominator; a threat quantity does not.** So the report contains a mishearing, established by logic alone.
- **Structural cross-check:** the model is a coherent two-stage pipeline — `F, K → [S = F × K] → S → [R = S/M] → R`, with the first matrix on axes (F, K) and the second on (S, M). Equation 1's output is equation 2's input. If the second matrix were on (risk, number of threats) the model would carry a redundant threat-counting step that breaks at zero.
- **Exam-safe formulation recorded:** `risk = frequency × consequences` (both must matter → multiply); `residual risk = risk ÷ protection factor` (protection ≥ 1, never divides by zero). This holds regardless of which letters the book prints.
- Still open: the paper notes, and the chapter PDF via library access.
