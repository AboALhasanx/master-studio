## OpenCode — OCR/VLM benchmark harness (performance evaluation of the free DeepSeek web transcriber)

- Student request: evaluate the performance of the free DeepSeek web-bot transcription feature, compare it with the PDF's search layer, and try unusual OCR engines.
- Built `90_Shared_Toolbox/tools/ocr_bench.py` (tracked, contains no book text). It extracts gold pages, builds hypotheses (PDF search layer, local RapidOCR), scores CER/WER with exact Levenshtein (numpy DP verified against brute force on 200 random pairs), splits the error into omission vs hallucination via a diff alignment, and counts **normalization drift** (printed typos silently "corrected" by the engine). Corpus data lives in the gitignored `_digitizer/bench/` tree.
- Gold = the 39 printed pages of Ch.1–2 that were proofread line by line in the previous step. Hypotheses scored on the same pages: (a) the archived pre-proofread DeepSeek web drafts, (b) the PDF's embedded OCR text layer, (c) local RapidOCR over the rendered page images (11.3 s/page, 439.6 s total).
- Result (39 pages, 93,638 gold characters): **DeepSeek web CER 1.038% / WER 0.827%**, PDF search layer CER 11.199% / WER 12.538%, local RapidOCR CER 19.493% / WER 21.510%. The free web VLM beats the book's own OCR layer by roughly 11x in character error and the local engine by roughly 19x. DeepSeek is at or below 0.11% CER on 33 of 39 pages; the residual pages are the ones carrying figures and tables, where the gold renders diagram content as structured lists while the model wrote prose descriptions — a representation difference, not a recognition failure.
- **Normalization drift is the dominant residual error mode.** All six remaining DeepSeek errors are printed misspellings the model silently corrected: `embarrasing`→`embarrassing` (p.3), `accomodate`→`accommodate` (p.10), `PRESOI`→`PRES01` (p.11), `complier`→`compiler` (p.12), `possiple`→`possible` (p.32), `interative`→`iterative` (p.38). The search layer and RapidOCR show zero drift — OCR is literal about spelling, the VLM is not.
- Tested the "repair the VLM output against the search layer" architecture directly. It does **not** work as an automatic corrector: a loose rule made 31 edits of which only a few were real, and CER got slightly worse (1.072%); a strict rule (same length, no substring, no digits, rare source token) made 4 edits and left CER flat at 1.040% with 5 drift events still open, because the layer's own OCR garbage (`developeq`, `dacument`) gets injected. The layer is usable as a **detector** of where the model normalized spelling (it flags the right pages), not as a **corrector**.
- Also measured, and rejected: agreement between two engines as a review-triage proxy. Agreement bands are not monotone in true error (the band with 90–95% agreement had a mean true CER of 0.01%, while 95–99% agreement reached 9.05%), because the search layer's own layout noise dominates the disagreement. An auto-accept rule at 99% agreement captured only 5 of 39 pages, so no meaningful funnel exists on this pair.
